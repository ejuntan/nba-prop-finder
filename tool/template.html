How often each player has cleared
      tonight’s line — not the line those games were played
      against.
      

    

      
Nobody stores a line for every player for every past game, and
      this tool has only kept its own since late October. So a record
      here is tonight’s number carried backwards: if this line had
      been up all along, how often would it have been beaten? That is
      the honest way to read a hit rate, and it is not the same question
      as whether the line is wrong.

      
A lopsided record usually means one of two things, and neither
      is an edge. The market may already have moved — a player on
      8-of-10 overs is often a player whose line has climbed three points
      since, so the old games clear a number nobody is offering any more.
      Or his role changed, and the recent games are a different player
      from the early ones; the Min column is there to show that.

      
Then there is the sample. Ten games of a coin flip gives eight
      or more the same way about one time in nine, so a 8–2 record
      on its own means very little. Beyond noise keeps only the
      records far enough from even to be unlikely from a 50/50 line, and
      even those are a starting point for a look, not a signal.

      
Where a book is quoting, the market’s line is used and marked
      book. Otherwise the number is the
      middle of his own last twenty, marked
      median: he cleared it half the time by
      construction, so a record against it describes his spread rather
      than anyone’s opinion of him. A window of ten or twenty reaches
      back across the season break and can include playoff games, which
      are a different sport for minutes — This season only
      cuts it to games since October.

    

  

  

    
    
  

  







  

  

    

      
      
      
      
    

    
Every market a book is quoting tonight, cut
      four ways and ranked by how often it has actually happened.
      

    

      
Each card is one quoted prop and one slice of the player’s own
      record against that number: his last ten games, every meeting with
      tonight’s opponent inside the logs, the games on this side of the
      fixture, and the games a team-mate who is out tonight also missed.
      The strip under each card is those games, oldest on the left, teal
      where the number was cleared and red where it was not. It is a
      picture of the games the card’s own record counted, so a card
      reading 8 from 8 shows eight marks. On an under, the side
      that keeps landing is the red one.

      
Two things these records are not. They are not a forecast —
      the line is tonight’s, carried backwards over games that were played
      against different numbers, so a perfect record often means the line
      has since moved to meet it. And they are not evidence on their own:
      three of three is what a coin does one time in eight, and a slice
      only has to clear three games to appear here.

      
Where fewer than three books are quoting a number, that number
      is one book’s opinion rather than a market. The Min books
      control is there to drop those.

    

  

  






  

    

      
      
    

    
Pairs built from the strongest records on the
      slate, priced with the correlation between them measured rather
      than assumed.
      

    

      
Every parlay calculator multiplies its legs, which assumes they
      are independent. Two markets on one player never are, and two
      players in one game are not either — the pace that makes one
      land makes the other likelier, while team-mates' shots come out of
      the same pool. So each pair here is measured on the games those
      players actually played, matched by date, and combined through a
      copula with that correlation in it. The line under each pair says
      which way it cut.

      
What these are not: a forecast. Each leg’s chance is that
      player’s own record at tonight’s number, carried backwards over
      games played against different numbers, and shrunk towards even
      because ten from ten is not certainty. A pair of strong records is
      a pair of strong records. Multiplying two of them does not make
      either more likely to repeat.
